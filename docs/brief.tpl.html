The short version

Build the Open Duck Mini v2 body unchanged and make the Atech board its brain. Open Duck Mini v2 is the Microduck's open predecessor by the same engineer (Apache-2.0, 129 print files, BOM, assembly guide, trained walking policies). Its electronics are a Pi Zero 2 W, a Feetech bus adapter and a BNO055 IMU, which are exactly the three things the Atech board plus its Robot Arm and Motion Sensor modules replace.

The Microduck features it lacks come from Atech modules: the same 8 × 8 VL53L5CX time-of-flight sensor, NeoPixel eyes, a MAX98357A speaker, a microphone. Its existing walking policy runs on the ESP32-S3 as-is, because the body does not change.

A 1:1 Microduck with XL330 servos is the less Atech-shaped route: the board does not fit an 82 × 64 mm trunk, the servos need a new Dynamixel 2.0 driver, and the meshes are licensed non-commercial. It stays on the list as a stretch goal.

What exists instead of a scan

Nobody has published a scan and Pollen's Onshape is view-only. Three things stand in for it, and the first is better than a scan would be:

  • The official meshes with the MJCF. 43 STL files exported from Pollen's own CAD, plus every joint origin, axis, range, mass and inertia. The assembler poses them in the STAND keyframe and writes one STL and a coloured GLB.
  • Community reconstructions. 70 parts already placed in world coordinates with a FreeCAD assembly, an electronics teardown read out of the Rust source, a fastener study, a combined GLB with the joint tree as JSON.
  • Open Duck Mini v2. 129 printable parts, Onshape, BOM, wiring and assembly guides, its MJCF with a calibrated STS3215 actuator model, and its Raspberry Pi runtime.

The community actuator study makes the point that settles the route: swapping XL330 for STS3215 is not a servo swap but a different robot, three times the servo mass with different cavities and a different actuator model. Open Duck Mini v2 is that robot, already designed and walking.

Three routes

A · recommended

Open Duck Mini v2 body, Atech brain

Print the 129 parts as they are. 14 × STS3215 at 7.4 V on a 2S 18650 pack. The Atech board replaces Pi, adapter and IMU inside the trunk; ToF, eyes and speaker go into the head on short cables. The trained walking policy transfers. About €295 of non-Atech parts.

B · stretch goal

1:1 Microduck with XL330

Print the official meshes, 15 × XL330-M288 at about €25 each, a new Dynamixel 2.0 driver on the same bus module, and a board that has to ride as a backpack on a 737 g robot. Every policy retrained. Non-commercial meshes.

C · not pursued

Atech-native duckling

Two DC motor modules and a duck shell as the case. A rolling toy that carries the Atech modules well but is not a Microduck.

Control contract

The firmware speaks the Open Duck Mini v2 runtime's contract so its policies transfer without retraining. A 50 Hz loop, an observation of 101 values: gyro, accelerometer, seven commands (vx, vy, yaw rate, four head angles), joint angles minus the standing pose, joint velocities scaled by 0.05, the last three actions, the motor targets, two foot contacts and a gait phase with a 0.54 s period. The 14 actions become targets as standing pose plus 0.25 × action; the head joints follow the command directly. Servo P gain 32 on the legs, 8 on the head, 2 when the body tilts past 60° so the duck goes limp instead of fighting the floor.

The exported policy is a 101→512→256→128→14 MLP with a tanh head, 218 k parameters, 853 KB of float32 in flash. The C header matches onnxruntime to 6 × 10⁻⁷. One forward pass is about 220 k multiply-adds, expected at 1 to 3 ms on the ESP32-S3 against a 20 ms budget. The BENCH command measures it on a real board.

Questions for Gustav

The work continues on the bold assumption in each line until you answer.

  1. Trunk fitDoes the 14-port board (60 × 120 × 7 mm plus about 20 mm of modules) go inside the Open Duck Mini v2 body, shared with two 18650 cells and three servos? Assumed yes, across the 150 mm width. Fallbacks: the 8-port board, or a backpack.
  2. Mass of board and modulesWeigh them on the Atech scale. Above about 80 g the centre of mass moves enough to matter.
  3. ServosBuy 14 × STS3215 in the 7.4 V variant, about €196? The Feetech STS3032 is Microduck-sized but no open body exists for it.
  4. IMU frameThe Atech IMU's axes versus the duck frame (x forward, y left, z up) and its mount rotation. A one-minute test once it is mounted.
  5. Which port pair is the module's UARTboard.yaml says ports 3 and 4; the Robot Arm driver notes say 10 and 11. Bench-check.
  6. The beak servoDropped in v1; Open Duck Mini v2 has a static beak. Add later on ID 34.
  7. CameraOut of scope on a microcontroller. An ESP32-CAM in the head is the only cheap route.
  8. Licensing intentPersonal build: anything goes. Atech product or marketing: stay on Open Duck Mini v2 (Apache-2.0) and do not ship the Microduck meshes.
